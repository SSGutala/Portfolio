Overview

Closet AI is a closet assistant. You photograph your clothes once and each item is tagged by category, color, and style with no manual entry. You tell it the occasion and the weather. It gives you a complete, ready to wear outfit.

Every recommendation comes from your own wardrobe. There are no shopping links and no outside suggestions. That constraint is the product.

Customer's Challenge

Our target customer is a young working professional who owns plenty of clothes they like and still freezes in front of the closet before a date, a happy hour, or a family dinner. It happens two or three times a week, and it usually ends with the same three shirts or a text to a friend.

It's not that I don't know what looks good. I just have too many options.

Customer interview, 500-item closet

The biggest thing we learned: the problem is not taste. It is choice overload. Friction scales with closet size and variety, so the more you own, the harder the decision gets. That makes it a decision relief problem, not a styling problem.

What did we ask?

  • How big their closet is and how much of it they wear
  • The last time getting dressed was stressful, and why
  • Who or what they turn to for a second opinion
  • Which wardrobe apps they tried, and when they quit
  • What a good recommendation would need to know about the day

We also read what people say about the apps already in this space. Two complaints repeat. Setup means photographing a hundred items before anything useful happens. And the suggestions feel random, or ignore half the closet.

Prototype and User Testing

The product works end to end. Here is one full run, from first open to a rated outfit.

  1. Onboarding: four cards explain scan, occasion, outfit, and rating.
  2. Sign in with email and password or Google.
  3. Add a clothing photo. The app analyzes it and fills in category, color, and style.
  4. Review the tags, fix anything, and save it to the closet.
  5. Pick what you are dressing for, then set how warm or cool it feels.
  6. Get outfits built from the closet and tap "I'll wear this".
  7. Rate the outfit. It lands in Outfit History with its stars.

What testing changed

  • Some requests came back with no outfit at all. The matching rules were too strict, so we relaxed them until the model can always return at least one.
  • Calls to the AI provider failed now and then during development. We added retry logic, which solved it.
  • Weather auto fill started as automatic. It became an explicit "Use my location" action so nothing is looked up without the user asking.
  • Shoes had no subtype, so weather and activity could not change the footwear. Flagged with the upload owner.

User Stories and Acceptance Criteria

Occasion Selection
O
As a user getting dressed for an event, I want to pick the kind of occasion, so that the outfit fits where I am going.
The app should offer a fixed occasion taxonomy and require one choice before continuing. The setting should default from the occasion type.
D
As a user, I want every occasion tied to a day, so that the outfit and the rating prompt match a real event.
The date should be required and default to today. Past dates should be blocked.
W
As a user, I want to give the weather quickly, so that I am not cold or overdressed.
The app should take a temperature in Fahrenheit or Celsius and reject out of range values inline. Conditions should be one of Clear, Cloudy, Rain, Snow, or Windy.
L
As a user, I want weather filled in from my location only when I ask, so that I stay in control of my data.
Location should be read only after the user taps "Use my location". Nothing should be pre-filled without that tap.
A
As a user, I want the outfit to match what I will be doing, so that I can move comfortably.
The app should offer Standing or Mingling, Seated, Active or Moving, and Exercise. When signals conflict, warmth beats activity and activity beats style.
Authentication
G
As a new user, I want to sign in with an account I already have, so that I can start without another password.
The app should support Google sign in and email with password. A first sign in should land on closet setup.
S
As a returning user, I want to stay signed in, so that I get to an outfit quickly.
The session should persist across visits and refresh without asking the user to sign in again.
R
As a user who forgot my password, I want to reset it, so that I do not lose my closet.
A reset link should be sent to the account email and the old password should stop working after reset.
X
As a user, I want to delete my account, so that my photos and data are removed.
Deletion should start a 30 day window, after which the account and its data are removed.
Outfit Quality
C
As a user, I want every piece in the outfit to be mine, so that I can wear it tonight.
Every recommended item should be validated against the user's closet. No shopping links and no outside items.

Risks and Remediations

Product Risk: users stall while cataloging, the same point where every wardrobe app loses people.
Auto tagging from one photo, with no manual entry. Hold marketing on the fast setup promise until the product clears it.
Technical Risk: strict matching rules return no outfit for small closets.
Rules relaxed so the model can always generate at least one outfit.
Technical Risk: the model recommends an item the user does not own.
Grounded validation and anti-hallucination guardrails check every piece against the closet.
Operational Risk: the AI provider fails mid request, or costs run away.
Retry logic on every call, plus a rate limit on model calls.
Scope Risk: Occasion Selection grew over several rounds into a larger feature than planned.
Forward date scheduling moved to its own PRD and is not in the current build.
Business Risk: an established app ships better AI styling.
The edge is setup speed and the segment we serve first, not the model.

  • The first 100 beta users come from the most active waitlist members.
What decides the price
  • Week 2 return rate: people who upload their closet and come back.
  • Cost per outfit generated, since every recommendation is a model call.
Target
  • 90 day plan: 1,500 waitlist signups and 100 active beta users. These are goals, not results.
